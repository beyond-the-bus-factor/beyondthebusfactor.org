The bus factor is a polite name for a harsh question. How many people would have to walk away before the thing you have built stops working?

For most teams the answer is one, and everyone knows who. It is the person who holds the deployment credentials, or the relationship with the biggest funder, or twelve years of context on why a decision was made the way it was. Nothing is written down, because writing it down was never the urgent thing.

This is rarely a technology problem. Projects and organisations come apart because the people carrying them leave, burn out, get ill, or take a job somewhere else, and nobody knows how to continue.

These resources came out of preparing for exactly that. In 2026 I spent three months off grid, out of contact, being ordained in the Spanish mountains. Getting ready meant working through every single point of failure in an open source project with tens of thousands of users, and finding out how many of them were me. What is here is what I wish had already existed.
