## The origin story

In 2026 I took a three month sabbatical in the Spanish mountains to be ordained in the Triratna Buddhist Order, after sixteen years of training. I was completely off grid, uncontactable, for the whole time.

Preparing for that meant confronting every single point of failure in the [Mautic](https://mautic.org) project. Who has access to what. How decisions get made without me. What the answer is if I do not come back.

Working through those questions turned up more gaps than I expected, and very little existing material to work from. Most of what I could find assumed either a corporate HR department or a foundation with staff. So I wrote what I needed, ran a workshop on it at GitHub Universe in October 2025, and other people started adding what they had learned the hard way.

## Who this is for

The resources here are being written to work in four settings.

**Open source projects.** Maintainers, release keys, governance, and what happens when the person who has always done the release stops answering.

**Charities and NGOs.** Trustee and board succession, funder and grant relationships held in one person's head, regulatory continuity, safeguarding leads, and the finance officer who is the only signatory.

**Companies.** Most already have a business continuity plan. Very few have one that covers key person knowledge risk, which is the part that actually takes six months to recover from.

**Small teams, collectives and volunteer groups.** Where one person carries most of the operation without anyone having counted.

The underlying failure is the same everywhere. The vocabulary is not, so each resource has an overlay that translates it into the language of your sector and adds the risks specific to it.

## The approach

None of this is theoretical best practice. It comes from transitions that really happened: maintainer departures, burnout, sudden absences, corporate spinouts, governance handovers, and the messy human business of keeping something alive through change.

Everything is released under CC0. Take it, adapt it, put your own branding on it, and do not credit me if that is easier. It works better when it is yours.
